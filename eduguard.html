ABOUT THE PROJECT

Problem: online exams need to catch real cheating without wrongly accusing honest students. A sneeze or a quick glance away should never flag anyone.

What I built: a time-series classification pipeline in Python. It aligns asynchronous video and system telemetry with timestamp-based nearest matching, handles missing data, engineers rolling-window features, then outputs a cheating probability for each session and applies a tuned 90% decision threshold.

Result: on the held-out test split, the tuned threshold reached 99.5% precision with only 2 false positives out of 2,000 test observations - evaluated with precision-recall curves specifically so honest students stay protected.

The live desktop app combines that trained model with sustained behavioral evidence (gaze deviation, elevated audio, tab switches) into one explainable risk score on a monitoring dashboard, with evidence messages saying exactly why risk increased.

KEY FEATURES

TEMPORAL ALIGNMENT

Video telemetry (every second) and system events (only when they happen) are merged with pandas merge_asof, the right tool for asynchronous logs.

ROLLING-WINDOW FEATURES

10-second rolling averages separate sustained anomalies from one-second noise like a sneeze or a quick glance.

IMBALANCE-AWARE TRAINING

Class-balanced Random Forest: with ~98% innocent data, a naive model scores high accuracy while flagging no one.

TUNED 90% THRESHOLD

The decision boundary is shifted from the default 50% to 90% probability, cutting false accusations to 2 in 2,000 test observations.

EXPLAINABLE RISK SCORE

The dashboard shows why risk increased - sustained gaze deviation, sustained audio or tab switches - not just a number.

LIVE MONITORING

Webcam gaze proxy, microphone level and tab-switch detection feed the model in real time in the desktop app.

HONEST LIMITS

The webcam signal is a gaze/head-angle proxy, not clinical eye tracking. The microphone value is a relative level, not physical dB SPL. The model was trained on engineered telemetry, and it does not detect phones, notes, hands or gestures. EduGuard is an assistive monitoring prototype: real use needs student consent, human review of flagged sessions and an appeal process.
